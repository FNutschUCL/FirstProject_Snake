# Build your own dashboard

A single page you'd actually want open every day. What goes on it is
entirely up to you — that's the real exercise.

Some things people put on theirs:

- the tasks or goals they're tracking this week, with progress
- a countdown to something that matters (a deadline, a trip, an exam)
- links they open every single day, in one place
- notes or a scratchpad that doesn't disappear
- habits, and how many days in a row they've kept them
- a reading or watch list
- their own schedule, typed in by hand

It does **not** need to connect to a real calendar or any outside
service. Typing things in yourself is completely fine — and if you ever
want to connect it to something real later, you now know who to ask.

---

## The three requirements

Every finished project must have all three. They're the same for both
routes.

### 1. It remembers something

Close the page, open it again, and what you entered is still there. This
is the one that makes it a tool rather than a demo.

### 2. Three parts that do different things

Not three lists. Three genuinely different things — for example a task
list, a countdown, and a notes area. Each doing its own job.

### 3. It looks how *you* want it to look

Not how the assistant first decided. At some point you must push back on
the design and ask for something different — bolder, calmer, different
colours, whatever you actually want.

---

## Before you call it finished

Use it as though it were a normal morning. Add something, close the tab
entirely, open it again. Is everything still there?

"It appeared to work once" is not the same as "it works".
