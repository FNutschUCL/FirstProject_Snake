Nobody Starts Knowing Help & common questions
Stuck?

Almost certainly not your fault.

Everything on this page has happened to someone else already, usually more than once. Find the thing that matches and you'll be moving again in a minute.

What am I actually looking at?

Your workspace has four parts. You only ever need three of them, and the fourth you can ignore entirely.

Far left strip
The icon bar

A column of symbols. Two matter: the pages icon at the top shows your files, and the robot icon opens Cline, your AI assistant. The rest you can ignore for this whole course.

Left panel
Your files

Everything in your project. You'll only ever open one or two of them, and mostly you won't need to open any.

Middle
Your game

The Snake game, running. When you make changes, this is where you see them.

Right panel
Your lessons

The course itself. Work down each lesson, tick things off, and use Next at the bottom to move on. The green Reload game button lives at the top of this panel.

Bottom (hidden)
The terminal

A black box for typing commands. It's deliberately closed and you never need it. If it opens by accident, close it with the ✕ in its corner.

One of the panels disappeared. How do I get it back?

The lessons: press F1, type Course: Open lessons and press enter.

The assistant: click the robot icon in the far-left strip, or press F1 and type Cline.

The files: click the pages icon at the very top of the far-left strip.

The game: it comes back with the lessons — use the Course: Open lessons command above.

Everything is squashed and I can't read anything

You can drag the dividing lines between panels to resize them. Grab the line between two panels and pull.

On a small screen, close the file list (click the pages icon to toggle it off) — that alone usually frees enough room.

The game

I changed something but the game looks exactly the same

This is the single most common one, and there are only three causes.

  1. You didn't press Reload. The green Reload game button at the top right of the lessons panel. The game doesn't refresh by itself.
  2. The file wasn't saved. If you edited by hand, press Ctrl+S (Mac: Cmd+S). An unsaved file shows a dot instead of an ✕ on its tab.
  3. The change didn't land. Tell the assistant plainly: "I reloaded but the game looks unchanged." Don't assume you did something wrong — this is a normal thing to report.
The game area is blank or white

Press Reload game first. If that doesn't do it, the change probably broke something.

Tell the assistant exactly that: "the game is blank after your last change, please undo it." It knows what it just did and can put it back.

The arrow keys don't do anything

Click on the game first. Keyboard presses go to whichever panel you last clicked, so if you were typing in the lessons or in Cline, that's where your arrow keys are going.

Click once on the game board itself, then try again.

I broke the game completely and want it back the way it was

Nothing here is permanent. Ask the assistant:

"Please undo your last change and put the game back the way it was."

If several changes have piled up and it's all a mess, ask it to go further back:

"Please restore game.js to how it was before we started this conversation."

The assistant

What is the Approve button, and do I have to keep clicking it?

Cline never changes your files without asking. Before each edit it shows what it plans to do and waits for you to press Approve.

That's a safety feature: nothing happens to your project that you didn't agree to, and you can stop a long task halfway if it's going the wrong way.

If the clicking gets tedious, turn on Auto-approve at the top of the Cline panel. It then carries out whole tasks without stopping. Most people switch it on after the first few changes.

Our suggestion: approve by hand at first so you can see what it actually does. Once that stops being interesting, turn it on.

It says it's done, but nothing happened

Press Reload game. That's it nine times out of ten.

If it really is unchanged, say so plainly: "I reloaded and it looks the same — can you check what actually changed?"

The assistant cannot see your screen. What you observed is the single most useful thing you can give it.

It's gone in circles three times and still can't fix it

Stop the loop and start a new conversation — the + at the top of the Cline panel.

A fresh conversation has no memory of the failed attempts, so it approaches the problem cleanly instead of defending its earlier answers. This works surprisingly often.

In the new conversation, describe the goal rather than the failures: what you want, not what went wrong.

It says I've hit a limit, or stops responding

You're on a free model, which has a cap on how much you can ask for in a period. Hitting it is normal on a big task and doesn't mean anything is broken.

Two things help:

  • Ask for smaller pieces. One change at a time instead of five.
  • Start a fresh conversation when you move to a new task, rather than carrying a long history around.

Otherwise, wait a while and try again.

It forgot everything we talked about yesterday

Expected. This assistant starts each conversation fresh — it has no memory of earlier ones. It can still read your files, so it knows the state of your project, just not the discussion.

Simply tell it what you're working on at the start. One sentence is plenty.

I can't find the robot icon

Press F1, type Cline, and choose the option to open it.

Two things to watch for: some icons hide behind a … at the bottom of the strip, and there may be a second assistant called Copilot on screen. Copilot is not the one this course uses — look for the robot.

Pages and tools you build yourself

It built something but I have no idea how to open it

Very common, and easily solved — just ask:

"I don't understand how to open the page you just made. Please give me the full web address I can click, and open it yourself in the editor next to my game."

The assistant built the file but has no way of knowing you can't find it. Telling it so is the fix.

It gave me a localhost address and it doesn't work

Right — and this one confuses everyone.

Your workspace runs on a computer in the cloud, not on your own machine. localhost means "this computer", so from your browser it points at the wrong place entirely.

Ask for the forwarded address instead:

"localhost doesn't work because this is a cloud workspace. Please give me the forwarded address for port 8080."

That gives you a long address ending in .app.github.dev, which does work.

My page opens but looks completely unstyled

Usually the styling lives in a separate file that isn't being found. Tell the assistant what you see:

"The page opens but has no styling at all — just plain text on white. Can you check the files are linked correctly?"

Where did my files go?

Look in the file list on the left. New files appear there as soon as they're created — you may need to open a folder by clicking the little arrow beside it.

If you can't spot it, ask: "where exactly did you put the files you just created?"

To avoid the problem next time, tell it where to work up front: "work in the folder final-project/game and create the files there."

When nothing works: starting over

In rough order of severity. Try them from the top — the first one fixes most things.

Level 1 — Reload the window

Press F1, type Developer: Reload Window, press enter.

This restarts the editor without losing anything. Your files, your progress and your project are untouched. It fixes panels that have got stuck or disappeared.

Level 2 — Reopen the lessons

If the lessons panel is gone or behaving oddly: press F1, type Course: Open lessons, press enter.

Your ticked-off tasks come back automatically — progress is saved in your project, not in the panel.

Level 3 — Rebuild the workspace

Press F1, type Codespaces: Rebuild Container, press enter. It takes a minute or two.

This rebuilds the environment around your files. Your work is kept. Use it if things are broken in a way a window reload didn't fix.

Level 4 — Start a completely fresh workspace

The nuclear option, and sometimes the fastest.

  1. Go to github.com and open the project repository
  2. Click the green Code button, then the Codespaces tab
  3. Find your existing workspace, click the … next to it and choose Delete
  4. Then click Create codespace on main to start clean
This deletes your changes

Anything you built in that workspace goes with it, unless you saved it to GitHub first. If you've built something you want to keep, ask the assistant to commit and push it before you delete anything.

Your course progress and certificate eligibility are not affected — those follow your account, not the workspace.

I accidentally closed my workspace tab

Nothing is lost. Go to github.com/codespaces, find it in the list and click it. It picks up exactly where you left off.

My workspace has disappeared from GitHub entirely

GitHub removes workspaces that haven't been used for a long time. If yours is gone, create a new one from the project — you'll start the course fresh.

To avoid this with anything you care about, ask the assistant to push your work to GitHub when you finish a session. Then it lives in your account rather than in a temporary workspace.

The course itself

Do I have to finish it in one go?

No. Close it whenever you like and come back — your ticked-off tasks and quiz answers are saved and the course reopens at the first lesson you haven't finished.

Most people do it across two or three sittings.

My progress disappeared

If you're in a different workspace than before — a new one, or a rebuilt one — that's why. Progress is stored in the workspace.

Nothing is broken and the certificate doesn't depend on it. Tick things off as you go past them and carry on.

Can I skip a lesson?

Yes. Next is never blocked and nothing is locked. If a lesson is too easy or doesn't interest you, move on.

The one worth not skipping is lesson 4, on checking the work. It's the one that stops projects quietly falling apart.

How do I get my certificate?

Work through to the last page and use the Get your certificate button. Type your name, and it downloads.

It's drawn in your browser — your name is never sent anywhere.

Do I have to take part in the research?

No, and it makes no difference to the course. Choosing "no thanks" gives you exactly the same lessons and the same certificate.

You can also change your mind later: go back to the first page and choose differently. Choosing "no" deletes everything collected up to that point.

Is any of this going to cost money?

No. The GitHub account, the workspace and the assistant are all on free tiers, and no payment details are needed at any point.

The only limit you might meet is the assistant's usage cap on a long task, which is a waiting matter and not a paying one.

None of this helped

Then it's worth an email rather than another twenty minutes of poking at it. Write to f.nutsch@ucl.ac.uk.

Useful to include: what you were trying to do, what you did, and what happened instead. A screenshot beats a description every time.

And genuinely — getting stuck is useful information for the research. You're helping either way.
